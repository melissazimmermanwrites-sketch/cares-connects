Growing up in foster care can mean moving a lot, changing schools and carrying more than anyone should at 15. [Children's Receiving Home of Sacramento](https://crhkids.org/programs/scc/) (CRH) has spent decades working with children, youth and families affected by abuse, neglect and trauma. Its campus is at 3555 Auburn Boulevard in Sacramento, and it describes 80 years of service to the region.

One program is aimed squarely at older teens and young adults. It's called Supporting Community Connections.

## Supporting Community Connections in Sacramento

Supporting Community Connections, or SCC, is a suicide prevention outreach program for transition-age youth in the foster, homeless and LGBTQ+ communities. The CRH site lists transition-age as ages 12 to 25. The program is grant funded.

In 2024 to 2025, CRH reports it served 298 youth from foster, homeless and LGBTQ+ communities.

## What SCC Offers Foster Youth

Staff provide individual case management and peer support. They also connect young people to housing, mental health services, women's health providers, LGBTQ+ resources and emergency supplies.

Groups include:

- SuicideTALK workshops, open to young people, staff and community partners
- Life Skills groups for foster and homeless youth
- Adolescent Social Skills and Self Advocacy, for foster youth living on campus
- LGBTQ Teen Support, open to transition-age youth
- Seeking Safety, a trauma and substance use curriculum for foster youth on campus

Some of these happen on the CRH campus and some in the community.

## How to Reach CRH's SCC Program

1. Call [916-616-2397](tel:+19166162397) or email [SCC@CRHkids.org](mailto:SCC@CRHkids.org).
2. Share what the young person needs, whether it's housing leads, someone to talk to, or a group.
3. For general questions about CRH, call the main line at [916-482-2370](tel:+19164822370).

The CRH site doesn't list formal enrollment rules for SCC. It names the young people it's meant for: foster, homeless and LGBTQ+ youth. Social workers can find resources on the CRH website as well.

## Other Programs at CRH

CRH also lists a Welcome Center, a residential treatment program, partial hospitalization and day treatment programs, and a trauma-informed preschool called Sprouts. Ask about them if you're a caregiver or caseworker.

## More Crisis Resources

SCC is prevention and support, not an emergency service. These lines are listed on the CRH site:

- 988: mental health emergencies
- Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741
- Trevor Lifeline: [1-866-488-7386](tel:+18664887386), or text START to 67678
- Trans Lifeline: [877-565-8860](tel:+18775658860)

Every young person deserves an adult in their corner. SCC is one way to find one, whether the need is a conversation with a peer who gets it, a link to housing, or a group where nobody has to explain why they're there. If you're a foster parent, relative or caseworker, you can call on a young person's behalf and ask what the next step looks like.
