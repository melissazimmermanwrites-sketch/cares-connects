If you don't have health insurance, a doctor's visit can feel out of reach, and small problems often grow while you wait. [Shifa Community Clinic](https://shifaclinic.org/) in Sacramento, California, is built for that situation. It is a student-run, nonprofit clinic that provides free, multilingual, multicultural healthcare to the uninsured in the Greater Sacramento area.

The clinic is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, and its website says it has seen 6,233 patients since 1994. Its mission is to bring communities that have been left out of the healthcare system into it, while giving future health professionals hands-on experience in caring for them.

## What Shifa Community Clinic Offers in Sacramento

Care at Shifa is free. Based on the clinic's website, services include:

- Primary care for uninsured adults
- Dental care, including extractions and fillings
- COVID-19 and flu vaccines
- Women's health, including exams, Pap smears and pregnancy tests
- Screening for diabetic retinopathy, an eye disease linked to diabetes
- Mental health support through the Mind Clinic
- Dermatology
- Podiatry for diabetic patients
- A legal clinic

Services can change, so ask when you call which ones are available the day you plan to come.

## Who Can Be Seen at the Clinic

Shifa serves people in Sacramento and the surrounding area who don't have medical insurance. You don't pay for your visit. You don't need a referral to call and ask. The clinic describes itself as multilingual, so mention the language you're most comfortable in when you make your appointment, and ask what help is available.

## How to Make an Appointment at Shifa

The clinic is open Sundays from 8 a.m. to noon, by appointment only. There is no walk-in care.

1. Call [(916) 441-6008](tel:+19164416008) or email info@shifacommunityclinic.org.
2. Tell them what kind of care you need.
3. Go to the clinic at 419 V Street, Suite A, Sacramento, CA 95818, at your appointment time.

Hours can change, so confirm them when you call.

## Good to Know

Shifa is a clinic that sees patients one morning a week, not an emergency room. If you have an emergency, call 911. For urgent needs between visits, an urgent care center or emergency department is the right place to go, and the clinic staff can talk with you about what to do next.

Students help run the clinic alongside working professionals, which is part of how the care stays free. The clinic's website also lists volunteer pages for undergraduates and for physicians, if you'd like to give your time.

Free care that treats you with respect can be the first step back into the healthcare system.
