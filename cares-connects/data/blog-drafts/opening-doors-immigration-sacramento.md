Immigration law is hard enough without a language barrier, a deadline and a bill you can't pay. [Opening Doors, Inc.](https://openingdoorsinc.org/programs/defending-immigrants/) offers free immigration legal services to people throughout the greater Sacramento region. Its office is on Howe Avenue in Sacramento.

The organization began in 1993 as the Sacramento Refugee Ministry. It became an independent nonprofit, Opening Doors, Inc., in 2002. Today it supports immigrants, refugees and survivors of trafficking, with the goal of stability, self-sufficiency and belonging.

## The Defending Immigrants Program in Sacramento

Defending Immigrants is the legal arm of Opening Doors. The program says its services are free. Representation in removal proceedings, which are cases in immigration court, is free for low-income people, including unaccompanied minors.

The program also offers individual consultations to prospective clients, along with community workshops and seminars.

## What Opening Doors Can Help With

The program lists help with:

- Asylum
- Adjustment of status (applying for a green card)
- Naturalization (becoming a citizen)
- DACA
- U visas and T visas
- VAWA petitions
- Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS)
- Representation in immigration court

If your situation isn't on the list, or you aren't sure which form applies to you, call anyway and describe what's going on. A consultation is the way to find out what fits your situation.

## How to Get Started With Opening Doors

1. Call [916-492-2591](tel:+19164922591), or use the form on the [Contact Us page](https://openingdoorsinc.org/contact-us/).
2. Ask for a consultation.
3. Visit the office at 1111 Howe Avenue, Suite 125, Sacramento, CA 95825.

The website doesn't explain the intake steps or say what languages are offered. Ask when you call.

## If Trafficking Is Part of Your Story

Opening Doors also runs a 24-hour human trafficking crisis hotline: [916-504-1118](tel:+19165041118). The National Human Trafficking Hotline is [1-888-373-7888](tel:+18883737888). If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.

## A Word About Trust

Reaching out about your status can feel risky. Opening Doors has worked with newcomers for more than 30 years, and its program is built to meet people where they are. You deserve accurate information from people who know the system.

Start with one call. The next step gets easier once someone is on your side.

## Who Is Behind the Work

Getting real legal help matters because immigration paperwork is unforgiving, and a missed deadline or a wrong form can change the outcome of a case. Opening Doors also runs programs for refugee welcome, survivor support, health navigation and economic opportunity, so a family can often find more than one kind of help in the same place. If you are helping a relative, a neighbor or a client, share the number and the address, and encourage them to ask for a consultation before any deadline passes.
