[Community Against Sexual Harm (CASH)](https://cashsacramento.org/) is a Sacramento nonprofit that supports commercially sexually exploited women and youth. Services include a drop-in center, emergency housing, case management, medical care and peer support from people who understand the road you're on.

CASH was created in 2008. It grew out of a community needs assessment that called for a safe, non-judgmental place where women could find support, referrals, food and basic care. That is still the heart of it, and the website describes the drop-in space as a place where you can eat, find clothing or rest without being asked to explain yourself.

## The CASH Drop-In Center in Sacramento

The Drop-In Center is in Oak Park, in Sacramento County. CASH lists only the general area on its website, so call before you go. You don't need an appointment to walk in.

The Drop-In Center is open Monday through Friday, 12:30 to 4:30 pm. The website says all are welcome to come in. You can get a meal, find clothing, or simply rest somewhere safe.

## What CASH Offers in Sacramento

CASH describes a wide mix of programs. Based on your situation, support may include:

- **Comprehensive Services for Survivors:** emergency housing, case management, recovery assistance and connections to health care
- **Support groups:** peer support for women of all ages
- **Housing support:** help working through homelessness and the options that exist
- **Peer mentoring and outreach:** street-level and online
- **Medical care:** a clinic run with Dignity Health, plus street medicine events
- **RESET:** a 25-hour court-offered course that meets prostitution diversion requirements
- **RECON:** a job readiness class for women looking for work

CASH doesn't publish the location of its emergency housing, and that's by design. Safety comes first.

## How to Reach CASH

Getting started is simple:

1. Call [916-856-2900](tel:+19168562900).
2. Tell them what kind of help you're looking for, or ask about an intake.
3. Or come to the Drop-In Center on a weekday afternoon.

You can also email [info@cashsac.org](mailto:info@cashsac.org). The website doesn't list formal eligibility rules, and it doesn't ask you to prove anything to walk in.

CASH serves women and youth, and some of its pages also speak of women and girls. If you're unsure whether you fit, the simplest answer is to call and ask, because the website says all are welcome.

## Why Survivor-Led Matters

Much of CASH's work is built on peer support. That means you may talk with someone who has lived through something similar. There's no lecture. There's no judgment.

In September, CASH worked with Dignity Health on a street medicine event that saw 27 patients for general medical care and tested 11 people for HIV, which shows how much of its work happens outside a building. CASH also leads a three-year research project to estimate the scope of sex trafficking in Sacramento County. And it has launched a capital campaign to build a larger, permanent center. Neither one changes what's available to you today.

If you're not ready to walk in, a phone call is a fine first step. If you're worried about someone else, the national hotline above can talk you through what to do next.
