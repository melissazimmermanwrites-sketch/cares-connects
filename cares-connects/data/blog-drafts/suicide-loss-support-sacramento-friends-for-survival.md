You lost someone to suicide, and the questions don't stop, while many of the people around you don't know what to say, which can leave you feeling very alone. [Friends for Survival](https://friendsforsurvival.org/) is a Sacramento nonprofit for exactly this kind of grief. It offers peer support to anyone grieving the suicide death of a family member, friend or loved one.

Friends for Survival has been doing this work since 1983. Its staff and volunteers have each lost someone to suicide themselves, so you're talking with people who understand the experience from the inside, and you don't have to explain or defend how you feel. The organization emphasizes that support is available whether your loss is recent or happened many years ago.

## Suicide Loss Support in Sacramento County and Beyond

The organization is based in Sacramento and runs meetings both in person and online. That matters when grief makes it hard to leave the house, get dressed or drive across town after a long day. The in-person meetings listed on the website include one in Roseville, held on the second Wednesday of the month from 5:00 to 6:30 pm. The rest of the schedule is online, so you can join from home.

These meetings are peer support, not therapy, and the website is clear about how they work. Here's what to expect when you join one:

- Meetings run about 90 minutes.
- You'll be in a group and may be placed in a smaller breakout room.
- What people share stays confidential.
- Passing is always acceptable, so you can simply come and listen.
- Specific methods of death aren't discussed in the group. The office offers one-on-one support for those conversations.
- Doors close 15 minutes after the start time, so please plan to arrive promptly.

## Groups for Different Kinds of Loss

The online schedule includes meetings for specific situations. Listings on the website have included groups for grieving moms, grieving men, spouses and partners, and a general group open to all loss survivors. There's also a meditation group and holiday events, such as a candle lighting in December.

Friends for Survival also runs small, guided grief groups that meet over several weeks. These are closed groups. You fill out an interest form and have a brief call with a facilitator first. Some of these cohorts carry a fee, and the website lists the price next to each one. The monthly drop-in meetings are free, except for one reconnect group for past long-term cohort members.

## Other Ways Friends for Survival Helps

- A free monthly newsletter called Comforting Friends
- A resource library and educational webinars you can watch any time
- Education and consultation for professionals who work with people who've lost someone to suicide

## How to Join a Meeting

1. Look at the meeting schedule at [friendsforsurvival.org/meetings](https://friendsforsurvival.org/meetings).
2. Pick a date and register using the link next to it. Drop-in meetings require registration, and same-day sign-up closes at 9 am Pacific.
3. Have questions first? Call [916-392-0664](tel:+19163920664) or the toll-free line, [800-646-7322](tel:+18006467322).

The website doesn't list a street address for the office, so call before you plan to visit in person.

Friends for Survival is not a crisis service, so if you're thinking about suicide, or someone you love is, call or text 988 first. When the worst of the crisis has passed, or if you're carrying a loss from many years ago, there is a room full of people who understand and who will not look away.
